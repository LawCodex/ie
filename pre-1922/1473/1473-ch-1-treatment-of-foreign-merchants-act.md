# Treatment of Foreign Merchants Act 1473

## 16 HENRY VL A.D. 1437-8.

Statutes published at Dublin in the sixteenth year of the reign of King Henry VI. before Richard [Talbot], Archbishop of Dublin, then Justiciar of the said lord the King of his land of Ireland.

Foreign merchants under protection of the King.

It is ordained that every foreign merchant of whatever condition or nation he be, who comes and arrives peaceably with his ships and merchandise in any port of this land, be in special protection of our lord the King, as well in returning out of the said land as while abiding therein. And if any take the said merchandise from their sailors or servant, their ships or boats, their goods or chattels, that the merchandise aforesaid be restored, by authority of the said parliament, and sued for by petition to the Justiciar of our lord the King, the lieutenant or his deputy for the [time] being, against those who take the said merchandise [from the] sailors, their ships, goods or chattels. And as soon as they are received upon the said petition, that proclamation be made in the presence of the said Justiciar, lieutenant or deputy, that they or he against whom the said petition is sued must appear before the said Justiciar, lieutenant or deputy, on a certain day to be appointed by the said Justiciar, lieutenant or deputy, and this under penalty of restoring to the said petitioner double the value of these goods and chattels, etc.
